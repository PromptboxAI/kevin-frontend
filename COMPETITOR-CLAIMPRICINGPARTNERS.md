## 1. What they are — and it is not what the last one was

**This is a human pricing desk, not software.** No app, no login, no platform.
You email photos or a folder link; people price the lines by hand and send back
an Excel workbook. Fully remote, run out of a 412 (Pittsburgh) number.

That makes them a **direct competitor to done-for-you**, and a far more serious
one than iContentsPro — because unlike a 10¢ line that cannot be built, **their
price is economically coherent.**

Their deliverable is close to ours on purpose:

| | Claim Pricing Partners | Kevin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Room-by-room Excel workbook | Room-by-room .xlsx + PDF |
| Provenance | Source link **plus a dated screenshot** | Source link |
| Uncertain lines | Flagged `CONFIRM` | Blank, editable `needs_manual` |
| Specialty items | "flagged for appraisal… We don't appraise" | Six classes never auto-priced |
| Turnaround | Under 300 lines in 24 hours | Processing is minutes; DFY is quoted |
| Carrier contact | "We never contact the carrier" | Same — we export, you submit |

## 2. The price, and why it is not the iContentsPro situation

| Lines | Kevin DFY | Them, $0.50/line | Them, $0.95 Xactimate | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | $750 | $50 | $95 | **15×** |
| 250 | $1,175 | $125 | $237.50 | 9.4× |
| 500 | $2,175 | $250 | $475 | 8.7× |
| 1,000 | $3,925 | $500 | $950 | 7.8× |
| 2,000 | $6,925 | $1,000 | $1,900 | 6.9× |

First 25 lines free. Corrections free. A rejected source re-sourced free in 24
hours ("Price Check guarantee").

**Check the labour maths before dismissing it.** At $0.50 a line, a person
working 1 minute a line earns $30/hour; at 2 minutes, $15/hour. Finding a retail
price for "Sofa, 3-seat, fabric", screenshotting the page and logging the link
is a 1–2 minute job. **So this is a real wage for real work** — comfortably so
offshore, and viable for a small US operation. It is not a fantasy number and it
will not collapse on contact with volume the way 10¢ a line does.

**Our $5.00 line rate is ~8–15× a viable competitor.** That is the finding.

## 3. But they are not competing with our actual product

The comparison above is DFY against them. The comparison that matters
commercially is **the subscription** against them, and it runs the other way:

| A PA doing this much a month | Them | Kevin Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500 lines | $250 | **$249** |
| 1,000 lines | $500 | **$249** |
| 2,000 lines | $1,000 | **$249** |
| 4,000 lines | $2,000 | $249 + credits |

**Break-even is 498 lines a month.** Past roughly one 500-line claim a month, a
Kevin subscription is cheaper than sending the work to them — and the gap widens
with every claim, because theirs is per-line forever and ours is flat.

Two more points in our favour on the same comparison:

- **They charge $0.95 a line — nearly double — for "Xactimate-compatible"
  contents.** We include the XactContents .xlsx in the subscription at no extra
  charge. Charging extra for the format the file exists to be read in is a soft
  spot.
- **They hand back a file; we hand back a system.** No re-pricing a line, no
  worksheet, no audit log, no second ingest session, no export history. Change
  your mind about a description next week and it is a new engagement.

**Recommendation: do not discount DFY at this.** Route the traffic to the
subscription, where we win on arithmetic rather than lose on it. DFY is for
people who will not use software at all; that is a different buyer and their
existence does not make $5.00 wrong. See §6 for the one page I would build.

## 4. The one thing they do better than us

**A dated screenshot of the source page, behind every line.**

This is worth taking seriously because it solves a problem we have already
written down. Rule 10 tells us to say "a listing", never "a live listing",
precisely because *links go stale over months*. Their answer is to capture the
page at the moment of pricing and ship the image with the workbook, on a
separate "Price Sources & Notes" tab kept off the main sheet.

For a line argued six months later, a dated screenshot is strictly better
evidence than a URL that now 404s. Theirs is manual; ours could be automatic at
the moment the engine prices a line, which is the one moment the page is
guaranteed to be live.

Their **Price Check guarantee** — carrier rejects a source, it is re-sourced
free within 24 hours — is the trust device built on top of it. We have the
re-price capability already; we have never made it a promise.

**⛔ REJECTED, owner, 2026-10-29. Do not build this.** The reasoning above is
wrong where it counts: link rot is real, but it is not a pain the buyer feels.
An adjuster who finds a dead source link simply re-researches the item — they
do not treat a dated screenshot of an old listing as binding, and it locks a
carrier into nothing. Twenty-two years in the field against an inference drawn
from a rule's phrasing; the field wins. Provenance is already served by the
Source Link plus the two correction routes in the product. **Nothing on this
site is a gap we should close.**

## 5. Scope they cover that we do not

They are a **whole-claim** pricing desk; we are contents. Their five service
groups: contents & total loss, structure & Xactimate, service estimates
(pack-out, storage, cleaning, mould/hazmat, restoration, debris removal),
depreciation & review (including checking the *carrier's* depreciation and a
written list of missing or underpaid lines), and ALE.

Most of that is deliberately not ours — cleaning was ruled out on 27 October,
and structure repair pricing is a different product. Two are worth a thought,
neither urgent:

- **Proof of ownership packets** — photos, receipts and statements organised
  behind each line. We have the photos and the lines; we have never shipped the
  packet.
- **Carrier estimate review** — their estimate against yours, missing and
  underpaid lines listed. $149 flat, or 9% of the approved increase with
  nothing owed if nothing is approved. That contingency structure is a smart
  offer, and it is adjacent to what our worksheet already knows.

They are unindexed and brand new. The giveaway is on their own pricing section:
**"Founding partners: 25% off for 90 days — for the first 10 public adjusting
firms, in exchange for a written testimonial."** They are buying their first
testimonials, which means they do not have any. A web search for the company
name returns nothing at all.

So: a credible offer, competently written, with no customers yet and no
published legal terms. Second company in a row with no Terms of Service and no
Privacy Policy — on a service you email claim photographs and homeowner PII to.

**The one page I would build:** something that owns "contents pricing service"
and "outsourced claim pricing" intent and does the §3 arithmetic honestly —
when to send it out, when to subscribe, and the 500-line break-even where the
answer flips. We already have the comparison-page form for this and a rule
against scoring competitors on guesses; this one needs no guesses, because
their pricing is published.
